# 0045 v0.9 corpus-expansion finding: the one-shot result of record on 30 synthetic single-function cases

Decision: The v0.9 result of record is the authoritative 30-case single-function run: 30 cases run 5 times each (150 runs) under one fixed config (cap=5, plateau_limit=2, model `claude-opus-4-8`, temperature 1.0), frozen at corpus HEAD 8b2dd60, recorded in reports/v0.9-dataset.json with the human-readable findings in reports/RESULTS.md. This run, not the earlier 5-case validation set, is what Darwin's one-shot finding (decision 0006) now rests on. The run was run-and-record only: no engine, scoring, gate, build_case, case, or dataset change was made to produce it. Four findings are adopted as the record:

1. One-shot in the general case. 143 of 150 runs (95.3 percent) reached the returned-best candidate in generation 1. The directed multi-generation loop is verified-present but NOT load-bearing in general at single-function scope. Its demonstrated value here is verification (the correctness and behavior gates), best-of-N retention, and the never-worse-than-baseline guarantee (decision 0002), not iterative feedback.
2. The loop's occasional role is variance, not shape. The 7 runs (4.7 percent) where a later generation produced the returned best fell in exactly three cases (int_to_words, parse_version, total_price) and are run-to-run stochastic recovery near the floor, not a property of function shape or degradation category. The same case is one-shot on some runs and loop-recovered on others (int_to_words best_gen [1,2,2,1,1]; total_price [1,1,1,2,1]), and the three cases span three unrelated categories. The earlier hypothesis that a validation/parse function shape makes the loop load-bearing was tested and REJECTED: parse_time, the case that suggested it in earlier validation, was one-shot on all 5 runs here (best_gen [1,1,1,1,1]). This record makes no shape-based or category-based claim about when the loop helps.
3. The authored floor is a 0.0 anchor, not a hard minimum. Five cases produced at least one run scored marginally below the 0.0 reference floor. Four (chunk, binary_search, rps_result, classify_triangle) are genuine: the candidate is dependency-free and genuinely simpler than the hand-authored reference, carries the `suspiciously-simple` flag, and is scored and returned as-is (the guard flags, it does not reject, per decision 0001). The floor is the clean-reference anchor, not a minimum the engine may not pass.
4. Structural-only scoring is gameable, disclosed not patched. The fifth sub-floor case, apply_discount, is the exception: its sub-floor score (-0.0588, identical on all 5 runs) comes from the hand-authored reference plus one line, `__all__ = ["apply_discount"]`, which suppresses the dead-code count for the lone public function without making the code simpler (its logical_loc actually rises by one). The dataset confirms the artifact directly (`confirmed_dead_code_suppression_artifact: true`). A non-structural metric trick produced a below-floor score with no genuine improvement, and it did so without tripping the `suspiciously-simple` guard. This is disclosed as a demonstrated limitation of structural-only scoring, reported and not patched, consistent with the project's trust-over-capability posture.

Context: v0.9 set out to convert the one-shot finding from a 5-case anecdote (decision 0006) into a result with statistical weight, on a synthetic corpus by construction (decision 0043) spanning seven degradation categories (decision 0044). The synthetic methodology contract fixed the claim, the category composition, the reference rubric, and the disclosure guarantees before any case was built, and required the full distribution to be reported including every case where the loop is load-bearing or the engine goes sub-floor. The authoritative run honors that: every number in RESULTS.md traces to the committed dataset, and no case was excluded after seeing a run on it.

Alternatives rejected:
- Report the corpus-expansion result as "real-world": rejected. The corpus is synthetic by construction (decision 0043); the headline limitation is exactly that, and the real-world claim is out of scope. The honest unit of the headline is the generation count, which does not depend on the doubly-synthetic magnitude span.
- Keep the validation/parse-shape hypothesis as a soft claim: rejected. The authoritative run tested it and parse_time was one-shot on all 5 runs; carrying a shape-based loop claim the data refutes would outrun the evidence (a violation of the project's claims-match-evidence convention).
- Patch the apply_discount `__all__` artifact (for example, by special-casing the dead-code detector) before reporting: rejected for this run. The run is run-and-record only; changing the scorer would change what is being measured. The gameability is disclosed as a finding about the scorer; closing it needs a non-structural detector or a gate process split, neither in scope here.
- Correct the four genuine sub-floor candidates back up to the floor: rejected. They are genuinely simpler and correct; rejecting a genuinely simpler correct answer would violate "objective only" (decision 0001). They are reported as-is with the `suspiciously-simple` flag doing its disclosed job.

Consequences: Darwin's one-shot finding is now grounded on 30 cases and 150 runs rather than 5, and decision 0006 is read through this record. All claims language is bounded to synthetic, single-function scope: no real-world, open-source, production, or emergent-self-improvement claim is made, and no shape-based or category-based claim about the loop is made. reports/RESULTS.md and reports/v0.9-dataset.json are the v0.9 results of record (RESULTS.md supersedes the prior 5-case RESULTS.md). The disclosed structural-scorer limitation (Finding 4) stands as an open, documented item, not a defect to hide. No optimization, scoring, equivalence, or default-lockdown behavior changed in v0.9; the milestone is corpus and documentation only (decisions 0043 through 0045).
