# 0035 P5.3b: artifact-backed multi-file variance regeneration

Decision: P5.3b ran the authorized six-run multi-file variance study live and regenerated the variance dataset from retained, committed snapshots. `python variance_harness.py run --n 6 --cap 5 --plateau 2 --model claude-opus-4-8` orchestrated six multi-file benchmark runs over the 3-case corpus, each writing its maggregate.json into a distinct committed snapshot path under reports/variance_runs/run_00 .. run_05, and `python variance_harness.py derive` then regenerated reports/mvariance.json and reports/MRESULTS.md from those committed snapshots. This completes F1's deferred half (decision 0026): the multi-file run-to-run variance dataset is now artifact-backed from retained snapshots rather than hand-recorded from printed mbenchmark output, and it supersedes the 2026-06-13 hand-recorded dataset. The numbers are fresh stochastic samples and differ from the 2026-06-13 ones; they are reported as they came out, not copied forward. The only code change is to variance_harness.py's note generation (_derived_note); the engine that produced the runs (mbenchmark, mloop, mgenerator, mevaluator, the evaluator, the sandbox container, the verdict-emission seam) is unchanged, which is what makes the new dataset a fair successor to the old. No version bump: this regenerates data at 0.6.0.

Run provenance (dated, recorded here rather than in the byte-stable artifact): 2026-06-17, model claude-opus-4-8, cap 5, plateau 2, N=6 runs over the 3-case multi-file corpus (doc_history, rate_limiter, task_scheduler), each candidate executed inside the v2.0 locked-down container (darwin-sandbox:py313), scored by the post-inverted-span-fix evaluator (decision 0013). Per-run measured spend: run_00 $0.291685, run_01 $0.370870, run_02 $0.378125, run_03 $0.335335, run_04 $0.296220, run_05 $0.462170; six-run total $2.1344. All six runs completed with full three-case snapshots; every run improved all three cases over baseline, with no baseline returned in any of the 18 case-runs.

Findings provenance: the stable_findings and unstable_findings arrays in reports/mvariance.json are auto-generated by derive (the _findings function), computed from the snapshot arrays, not hand-curated prose. The regenerated dataset therefore carries derive's two computable stable facts (the improved-over-baseline and baseline-returned counts, and the generation-1 span range) and its two unstable facts (the all-three-one-shot count, and the feedback_gain_pp range), each re-verified against the new arrays. The floor-relationship narrative that the 2026-06-13 mvariance.json carried as a third hand-curated stable finding is not something derive computes; that narrative lives in MRESULTS prose and is re-derived here from the snapshots' best_bloat_removed_pct and best_flags.

Comparison of the new findings to the 2026-06-13 dataset:

Held:
- Zero regressions. Every run improved all three cases over baseline, the baseline original was never returned, and 18 of 18 case-runs improved. Held verbatim in both datasets.
- Generation 1 does the bulk of the work. Generation 1 alone closes the large majority of each case's original-to-floor span on every case and run. Held qualitatively.
- One-shot versus needs-feedback flips run to run; the later-generation contribution is opportunistic, not dependable. Held, and slightly stronger in the new draw.
- The references are soft: returned bests sit at or below the reference floor, so bloat-removed above 100 percent measures reference looseness, not candidate excellence. Held, and strengthened (see the floor count below).

Shifted, with the specific differences:
- Generation-1 span range: 79 to 104 percent (old) became 87 to 114.9 percent (new). Both ends moved up; the new minimum is rate_limiter run_00 at 87.0 percent and the new maximum is doc_history run_04 at 114.9 percent.
- All-three-one-shot runs: 1 of 6 (old) became 0 of 6 (new). No run had all three cases reach the floor in generation 1; the new draw is more feedback-leaning.
- Per-case one-shot rate: doc_history 3/6 became 2/6; rate_limiter held at 1/6; task_scheduler held at 2/6.
- feedback_gain_pp overall maximum: 24.02 (old) became 20.47 (new). Per-case ranges: doc_history 0 to 24.02 became 0 to 20.47; rate_limiter 0 to 22.48 became 0 to 15.11; task_scheduler 0 to 10.81 became 0 to 13.14 (task_scheduler's upper end rose).
- Floor relationship: 17 of 18 case-runs at or below the floor with one above-floor exception (task_scheduler one-shot at 98.2 percent) (old) became 18 of 18 at or below the floor with no exception (new). The single above-floor exception did not recur. Of the 18 new bests, 14 carried a below-floor flag (suspiciously-simple twelve times, fragmentation-smell twice).
- New-data nuance on feedback materiality: one best-generation-2 run (doc_history run_04, "run 5" in the 1-based MRESULTS numbering) gained 0 pp because generation 2 only tied generation 1's score and was selected by tie-break. So a best-generation count of 2 or more overstates load-bearing feedback; the gain magnitude (the decision 0021 materiality measure) is the honest signal, and the feedback_gain_pp range starting at 0 captures these tie cases.

Note-design choice: _derived_note now emits the durable framing built only from values read out of the snapshots: the one-sample caveat (a single live run is one stochastic sample, not the result), the model, config, and N, the artifact-backed provenance (derived from N committed maggregate.json snapshots under reports/variance_runs/), and the reproducibility note (re-run derive on the same snapshots for a byte-identical dataset). It carries no wall-clock date on purpose: a date would make a re-derive on the same snapshots differ, defeating the reproducibility guarantee. The byte-identical guarantee was proven, with two consecutive derives producing an identical sha256 and an empty git diff on reports/mvariance.json. The terse one-liner note that P5.3a left in place was replaced, not appended. The dated run provenance lives in this decision record, which is the right home for a wall-clock fact.

Alternatives rejected:
- Keep the 2026-06-13 hand-recorded numbers: rejected. The entire point of F1's deferred half is to replace hand-recorded numbers with artifact-backed ones; the machinery existed since decision 0023 and only the authorized model-spend run was outstanding.
- Carry the 2026-06-13 findings prose forward verbatim: rejected. The new runs are fresh stochastic samples and several numbers shifted, so copying old sentences would misstate the data. Every finding is re-derived from the new snapshots, and the qualitative story is reported as it actually came out.
- Put the run date in the auto-note: rejected. It would make derive non-byte-identical on re-run, defeating the reproducibility guarantee. The date is recorded here instead.
- Vary the config or touch the engine to shape the numbers: rejected and out of scope. The config (cap 5, plateau 2, claude-opus-4-8) is fixed to match the prior dataset, and the engine is unchanged, which is exactly what makes the new dataset a fair successor and the comparison meaningful.
- Bump the version: rejected. This regenerates data; it is not a release. The version stays 0.6.0.

Consequences: reports/mvariance.json and reports/MRESULTS.md now carry artifact-backed 2026-06-17 numbers derived from six committed snapshots under reports/variance_runs/run_00 .. run_05; a re-derive on those snapshots is byte-identical, so anyone with the repo can regenerate the dataset with no spend. F1 is fully closed: its configuration and determinism (decision 0022) and its reproducibility machinery (decision 0023) were delivered in v0.5, and its artifact-backed regeneration is delivered here. The committed snapshots are the retained artifacts that the 2026-06-13 dataset lacked. The pytest CVE bump (P7.2) and all other v0.7 work are untouched; the next milestone remains v0.7 (test and CI maturity). No optimization, scoring, gate, generation, sandbox, or seam behavior changed; the only code touched is variance_harness.py's note generation.
