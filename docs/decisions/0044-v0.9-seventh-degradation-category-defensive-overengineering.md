# 0044 v0.9 seventh degradation category: defensive_overengineering, added by dated amendment ~~before its cases were built~~ (ordering corrected 2026-06-21: the amendment was recorded after 2 of its 5 cases; see Correction)

Decision: A seventh degradation category, `defensive_overengineering`, is added to the v0.9 corpus taxonomy, which previously fixed six categories (`verbose_patterns`, `duplication`, `excessive_nesting`, `over_abstraction`, `unnecessary_dependency`, `dead_code`). The category covers simple correct logic wrapped in excess validation: per-element isinstance guards, try/except around arithmetic that cannot raise, None guards on values that cannot be None, and redundant re-validation after a computation. The addition was made as Amendment 1 to the synthetic methodology contract (docs/darwin-v0.9-corpus-methodology-synthetic.md, dated 2026-06-20) and committed at 2f2cd74 (2026-06-20T23:29:59Z) ~~before any `defensive_overengineering` case entered the corpus~~ [ordering corrected 2026-06-21; first_or_default and normalize_scores already carried the category at 96a681d, 2026-06-20T16:38:33Z, about 6h51m earlier; see Correction].

Context: The methodology contract fixes the category composition before building precisely so the corpus cannot be assembled after the fact to flatter the finding (decision 0043). During corpus authoring a seventh distinct anti-pattern surfaced that none of the six captures cleanly: excess defensive validation. It is structurally distinct from `dead_code` (the guards are reachable, not dead) and from `over_abstraction` (the excess is defensive, not structural layering). Leaving it inside one of the six would have mislabeled the cases; adding it silently would have broken the contract's guarantee that the taxonomy on disk is fixed before building. The amendment route records the change on the record, with a date and a rationale. ~~The amendment route preserves the guarantee: the contract is changed on the record, with a date and a rationale, and the change lands before the cases it describes. Alternatives rejected:
- Force the pattern into `dead_code` or `over_abstraction`: rejected. The guards are reachable (so not dead code) and the excess is validation rather than abstraction layering; either label would misdescribe the cases and weaken the taxonomy.
- Add the category silently, without an amendment: rejected. That would violate the contract's pre-commit-before-building guarantee and the milestone's standard that a changed methodology is recorded, not hidden.
- Drop the cases that did not fit the six and keep the taxonomy frozen: rejected. The excess-validation pattern is a real and common degradation; excluding it to protect a frozen list would make the corpus less representative for a bookkeeping reason.

Consequences: The v0.9 corpus spans seven categories, not six. In the authoritative 30-case run, `defensive_overengineering` is carried by 5 cases (apply_discount, clamp, compute_stats, first_or_default, normalize_scores; reports/v0.9-dataset.json, reports/RESULTS.md). ~~The integrity guarantee that the contract is fixed before building is preserved via the dated, committed amendment rather than a silent edit;~~ [corrected 2026-06-21; the fixed-before-building discipline was in fact violated for 2 of the 5 cases, and the guarantee that holds is freeze-before-run, not contract-fixed-before-building; see Correction] the amendment text is retained in the contract under "Amendment 1". One of the seven, apply_discount, is also the case that exhibits the disclosed structural-scorer limitation (decision 0045, Finding 4). No engine, scoring, or gate behavior changed: this is a taxonomy-and-authoring decision recorded after the fact.
